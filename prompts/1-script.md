5. Include natural disagreements, misunderstandings, questions and humorous corrections between the speakers.
6. Avoid sounding like a classroom lecture, textbook or formal presentation.
7. Make the conversation flow naturally, with short reactions and engaging exchanges.
8. Explain difficult concepts in simple language without compromising accuracy.
9. Use real, verifiable information. Never invent facts, statistics, quotations or historical details.
10. When appropriate, distinguish commonly confused terms, concepts or misconceptions.
11. Include practical takeaways that listeners can remember and apply.
12. Avoid unnecessary repetition and generic motivational speeches.
